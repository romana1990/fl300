I. The facts

The cake was ordered by telegram for a gentleman's thirtieth birthday, to be delivered at the end of the line.

00:40 — The train stops at Vinkovci. 00:58 — The conductor sees a man in the uniform of the Wagons-Lits, whom none of the staff know, step down on to the platform. He does not come back.

01:05 — Past Vinkovci the train runs into a snowdrift and stops. It has not moved since.

01:15 — The chef sets the cake, whole and untouched, on the last table of the dining car, and goes to bed.

06:30 — The steward finds it stabbed twelve times.

The snow around the train is smooth and unmarked. Since Vinkovci no one has left the train, and no one has come aboard.

Found behind the dining-car curtain: a Wagons-Lits tunic belonging to none of the staff.

II. Report of Dr. Konstantin on the cake

  1. A deep thrust, straight down through all three tiers to the plate. Great strength.
  2. A slanting cut, drawn from left to right: a left hand.
  3. A shallow nick. The blade went in, stopped, came out, went in again beside it. Hesitation.
  4. A steep thrust into the very top, from high above the table. A person of ordinary height could not have managed the angle without standing on the bench, and the bench is undisturbed.
  5. In this cut, traces of strong black coffee.
  6. Not a knife: four small holes in a row. The tines of a dessert fork of the Compagnie.
  7. A clean cut, its edges stained violet. Ink.
  8. Not a stab at all. A bite. A large one.
  9. In the wound, a strand of red wool.
  10. A cut placed at the exact geometric centre of the bottom tier, to the millimetre.
  11. A pinch of red powder in the wound. Paprika.
  12. Beside the last mark, carved neatly in the icing: XII.

Twelve marks, no two alike. — K.

III. The passengers

Compartments with a communicating door are marked ⇄.

Milos No. 1 ⇄ 2

“Ema and I were together all night with the door open between us. Neither of us went out. About one o'clock I heard a man in the corridor. In uniform, I am sure of it.”

Must stoop under every doorway in the carriage. The tallest passenger aboard.

Ema No. 2 ⇄ 1

“Milos never left. He was snoring through the door, I can promise you that.”

Signed her statement with her left hand.

Danica No. 3 ⇄ 4

“Nikola and I played cards until it was light. Neither of us left the table, and he cheated the whole time.”

Seven cups of black coffee at dinner; asked for an eighth “for later”.

Nikola No. 4 ⇄ 3

“I was with Danica all night. Cards. I did not cheat.”

Lifted the conductor's trunk on to the rack with one hand.

Aleksa No. 5 ⇄ 6

“Danica and I talked until four. The only thing we heard was that fellow in uniform going past.”

Ate two dinners, then asked whether there would be dessert.

Danica No. 6 ⇄ 5

“Aleksa did not leave. I sat knitting by the door; nobody could have gone past me.”

Knitting a long red scarf.

Aleksandra No. 7 ⇄ 8

“Ivan and I were reading. Neither of us went anywhere.”

In her handbag, a dessert fork of the Compagnie. “A souvenir,” she says.

Ivan No. 8 ⇄ 7

“Aleksandra was with me. We read the timetable and argued about it.”

Carries a slide rule. Corrected the conductor's arithmetic twice.

Nevena No. 9 ⇄ 10

“Peđa and I talked through the door until we both fell asleep. He never went out.”

Took eleven minutes at dinner to choose between the fish and the veal, then changed her mind.

Peđa No. 10 ⇄ 9

“Nevena never left her compartment. I would have heard the door.”

Writes everything with a leaking fountain pen; violet fingers.

Mikan No. 11 ⇄ 12

“Zorana and I sat up all night talking about Belgrade. Nobody left. I did see the man in uniform, though.”

Carries his own tin of paprika and puts it on everything, breakfast included.

Zorana No. 12 ⇄ 11

“Mikan was with me the whole time. I will swear to it. In Latin, if you prefer.”

Numbers her pages, her suitcases and her teacups in Roman numerals.

There are always two solutions. One is for the police.
